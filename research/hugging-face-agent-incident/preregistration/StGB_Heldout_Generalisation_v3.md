# StGB held-out generalisation protocol v3

Status: sealed pre-registration. No held-out scenario has been executed. Article publication remains on hold.

## Question

The v2 differential answered a decision-equivalence question. It showed five Operome changes against an endpoint ACL, three against exact object capabilities and zero against a contextual policy fitted with the ten scenarios visible.

Version 3 tests a different question prospectively: without editing either frozen system, does the source-derived StGB catalogue provide more correct explicit coverage for mechanism-novel conduct within its statutory boundaries than the frozen incident-authored contextual policy?

The negative control asks whether the Operome declines to match conduct involving a genuinely different protected interest. A stretched or analogical match is scored as an overreach false positive, not coverage.

## What is frozen

The registration freezes and hashes:

- the v2 endpoint, object and contextual policy objects;
- the complete v2 policy-pack file;
- external StGB corpus version `stgb-operome-2026-06-10`;
- the machine-overlay marked source and derived XSD;
- the machine evaluator and contextual-policy runtime.

The external legal corpus predates the July 2026 incident. The forty-profile machine-applicability overlay does not: it was assembled on 8 August 2026. The experiment therefore tests prospective generalisation from the registration timestamp. It cannot establish that the machine overlay existed before the Hugging Face incident.

## Construction disclosure

The v2 contextual policy was written after the incident disclosure with the ten scenarios and relevant Operome profiles visible. Its zero marginal result is a fitted decision-equivalence control. It is not evidence that a security team would independently have written the same rules before encountering the conduct.

Version 3 does not compare rule-language expressiveness. It compares explicit frozen-rule coverage under novelty. A global default deny is recorded separately as `default_only` and is never counted as semantic coverage.

## §202c correction

Historical scenario `IR2-004` is quarantined from law-derived coverage claims. Its description established that an authorised programme was selected for an offence-purpose task. It did not establish that the programme itself had the objective offence-purpose required by §202c(1)(2).

For future scoring, `PurposeBuiltPrograms = true` requires evidence of developer design, objective programme characteristics or an approved legal ruling. Current use, declared task purpose and mere suitability are rejected bases. The v2 files remain unchanged as historical evidence.

## Frozen held-out design

The held-out denominator is twenty scenarios:

- ten mechanism-novel scenarios within an existing statutory interest;
- five protected-interest-novel negative controls;
- five legally unresolved scenarios.

The candidate source corpus must be registered and completely inventoried before scenario construction. A source curator, scenario author, contextual adjudicator, German-law reviewer and deterministic runner must be distinct. No scenario may be removed or replaced after evaluation begins.

The test remains a safe local simulation. All targets must use `mock://`; model, network, process, credential, exploit-payload and production-filesystem activity are prohibited.

## What will be measured

Primary measures:

- explicit contextual-rule coverage for the ten mechanism-novel scenarios;
- correct source-bounded Operome-profile coverage for the same scenarios;
- the paired coverage difference;
- the Operome overreach false-positive rate on the five protected-interest-novel controls.

Catalogue availability and automated routing are separate results. A reviewer-supplied section identifier cannot be reported as successful automated profile selection. Fact extraction and adversarial observability also remain outside this protocol.
